-We study the Cauchy problem for a massive, charged Rarita--Schwinger (spin-$3/2$) field with a Pauli coupling, on the fixed background $\R_t\times S^2(1)\times S^2(3)$ threaded by a parallel magnetic field. The analysis is local, near one point. At one explicit parameter point we prove that no constants $K,\alpha$ exist for which existence, uniqueness and a loss-free $L^2$ energy bound all hold on small cones. The field equations are reduced exactly to an evolution equation for the spatial components, with a single constraint that the reduced operator maps into itself. A constrained, variable-coefficient necessity lemma shows that such a package would force a resolvent bound for the principal symbol on the kernel of the constraint symbol. That bound is contradicted by an exact Jordan chain at a real eigenvalue, with entries in $\Q(i)$. The constraint for the time component is solvable, and every characteristic speed at the chosen covector is real. We do not identify which of the three properties fails, and we make no global or physical claim. All computations are exact and public.

-The Rarita--Schwinger equation \citep{RaritaSchwinger1941} describes a field of spin $3/2$. Coupling it consistently to other fields has been difficult from the start. Buchdahl studied the compatibility of the equations in a gravitational field \citep{Buchdahl1958}. Velo and Zwanziger showed that the minimally coupled, charged field in an external electromagnetic potential propagates non-causally \citep{VeloZwanziger1969}. Inconsistencies in homogeneous magnetic fields were also found \citep{SeetharamanPrabhakaranMathews1975}. Many remedies and reformulations followed: modified wave equations \citep{Khalil1977}, supergravity \citep{DeserZumino1976}, and non-minimal couplings and their causality bounds \citep{DeserPascalutsaWaldron2000,DeserWaldron2002,PorratiRahman2009,Rahman2013}. Recent work revisits charged spin-$3/2$ fields in constant and Einstein--Maxwell backgrounds \citep{BenakliDanielKe2023,DelplanqueSkvortsov2024,Benakli2026,KrawczykEtAl2026}. The Velo--Zwanziger phenomenon itself has been revisited too \citep{Frauendiener2003}.
